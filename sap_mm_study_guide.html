Slides 1–3 What SAP is and why it dominates
▼

SAP stands for Systems, Applications and Products in Data Processing. Founded in 1972 by five ex-IBM engineers in Germany. It is the world's leading ERP platform — the software companies use to run their entire operations from one system.

OUTDATED — slide 3 figures

Revenue "$19B", 74,400 employees, 291,000 customers are all old (~2013 era). Current figures: $36B+ revenue, 105,000+ employees, 400,000+ customers. Don't memorize the numbers — memorize the dominance.

The key stat that still holds: 74% of the world's transaction revenue touches an SAP system. That's why knowing SAP is a career asset regardless of which module you work in.

▼

The 2013 Gartner chart shows SAP at 24% market share vs Oracle 12%, Microsoft 5%, Infor 6%, Sage 6%, others 37%.

OUTDATED — 2013 data

This entire chart is pre-cloud. The ERP landscape shifted dramatically since 2013. Workday emerged as a major HR/Finance cloud ERP. Oracle Cloud grew. Microsoft Dynamics 365 expanded significantly. The market is now cloud-first — but SAP is still #1.

Slide 5 SAP's product evolution timeline
▼
1970s

R1 — Stand-alone mainframe

First SAP product. Ran on a single mainframe. Very limited integration.

1980s

R2 — Mainframe

Improved mainframe system. Still centralized, no distributed architecture.

1990s

R3 — Client/Server

The breakthrough product. Moved to client-server model. Introduced the modular architecture (MM, FI, SD, PP...) that still underpins SAP today.

2000s

ECC — NetWeaver (SOA)

ERP Central Component on NetWeaver platform. Service-Oriented Architecture. Where most companies still are today (being migrated off).

2015+

S/4HANA — In-memory + BTP (cloud)

Current product. HANA in-memory database, Fiori UI, SAP BTP as the tech platform. The deck doesn't cover this — but this is what you're actually being trained for.

OUTDATED

The slide ends at ECC/NetWeaver as if it's current. S/4HANA launched in 2015 and is the active product. NetWeaver is legacy. SAP BTP replaced NetWeaver's role as the technology platform.

Key MM integrations — memorize these:

MM ↔ FI

Every goods receipt and invoice verification automatically creates accounting documents in FI. No manual entry needed.

MM ↔ PP

Production Planning triggers MRP, which generates purchase requisitions in MM for materials needed.

MM ↔ SD

Sales orders in SD consume inventory tracked in MM. Availability checks run against MM stock data.

MM ↔ QM

Goods receipts can trigger quality inspection lots before stock is released for use.

S/4HANA STATUS

All these modules and their integrations carry over identically into S/4HANA. The abbreviations, the cross-module flows, and the logic are the same. What changed: the database is HANA (in-memory), the UI is Fiori, and some backend tables restructured. The functional behavior is the same.

Slides 9–11 What ERP is — core concepts
▼

ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) is business management software that integrates all company functions into one system with a central database. Before ERP, every department had separate software that didn't talk to each other.

5 ERP characteristics — all apply directly to MM:

Integrated system

A GR (Goods Receipt) in MM auto-creates an accounting document in FI. Zero manual re-entry between modules.

Real time

Stock levels update the moment you post a goods movement. No batch delays. Inventory is always live.

Best practice

SAP enforces the P2P process: PR → PO → GR → Invoice → Payment. You can't skip steps without specific config.

Customizing (config)

MM is heavily configurable in IMG — plant parameters, valuation classes, movement types, number ranges, etc.

Process oriented

MM is built around Procure-to-Pay. Not around individual functions — around end-to-end business processes.

ERP components simplified (slide 11):

Enterprise

A firm/company that buys, makes, sells, ships products or services

Resource

Factors of production — Land, Labor, Capital. In MM context: materials and inventory

Planning

Organizing activities to achieve a goal. In MM: MRP, procurement planning, stock planning

Integration

The whole point — one database, every department, no silos

Slides 12–13 ERP evolution — why MRP history matters for MM
▼
1970s

MRP — Material Requirements Planning

Factory material inventory. Emphasis on physical assets. Calculate what materials to order and when. This is the direct ancestor of SAP MM's MRP functionality.

1980s

MRP II — Manufacturing Resource Planning

Expanded beyond materials to all factory resources — machines, labor, capacity. Added production scheduling.

1990s

ERP — Enterprise Resource Planning

Added departmental modules: HR, general ledger, purchase orders, analytic CRM. Moved toward intangible assets. This is when SAP R3 was the dominant player.

2000s

ERP II — Extended ERP

ERP + external actors. Added CRM, SCM, and other stakeholder modules. Global systems. SAP Business Suite era.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR MM

SAP MM's MRP (transactions MD01/MD02) is the direct descendant of the 1970s MRP concept. When you run MRP in SAP, you're executing the same logic: calculate what materials are needed, when, and in what quantities based on demand. Understanding this history makes MRP click faster. MM didn't appear from nowhere — it's 50+ years of manufacturing planning logic baked into software.

Slides 15–18 Value chain — where MM lives in a company
▼

Porter's Value Chain divides a firm's activities into Primary (value-creating) and Support (enabling) activities. Value Added minus Cost equals Margin. Understanding this tells you exactly why MM exists.

ActivityTypeMM role
Inbound logisticsPrimaryMM owns this — receiving, raw material control, quality control on receipt, warehouse management
OperationsPrimaryMM supports via inventory availability for production. Links to PP module.
Outbound logisticsPrimaryFinished goods, delivery, invoicing — primarily SD, but MM tracks finished stock
Sales & marketingPrimaryMostly SD, but stock availability checks hit MM data
ServicesPrimaryWarranty, maintenance parts — PM module, uses MM for spare parts
ProcurementSupportMM owns this entirely — supplier management, purchase orders, specification, subcontracting
HR ManagementSupportHR module
Tech DevelopmentSupportIT/R&D — PM, PS modules
InfrastructureSupportLegal, accounting, financial management — FI/CO modules

KEY TAKEAWAY

MM controls the left side of the value chain — where physical goods enter the company. Inbound Logistics + Procurement support activity = MM's domain. This is your justification in any business conversation for why MM is critical. No materials in = no production = no revenue.

MM organizational structure — know which level each lives at:

Client

Top level. One SAP system. All data below inherits from client.

Company Code

Legal entity. Financial statements produced here. Vendor master has Company Code data.

Plant

Production/warehouse location. Material Master has plant-level data (MRP, purchasing views).

Storage Location

Physical storage area within a plant. Stock is tracked here.

Purchasing Org

Negotiates with vendors. Vendor master has Purchasing Org data.

Purchasing Group

Buyer/team responsible for specific materials. Assigned on PO line items.

Key MM master data:

Material Master

Central record for every material. Views include: Basic Data, MRP, Purchasing, Plant Data/Storage, Accounting, Costing.

Vendor Master

Supplier data. Three levels: General Data (client), Company Code (FI), Purchasing Org (MM).

Purchasing Info Record

Links material to vendor with pricing. Auto-populates POs. Transaction ME11.

Source List

Defines approved vendors for a material at plant level. Can enforce single sourcing.
